name: problem
description: "Find the real problem behind an idea or request. Use when someone says 'I have an idea', 'should I build X', 'what's the real problem here', or brings a solution without saying what it solves."
---

Call the Skill tool with "lexicon" if it isn't already loaded.

Ideas usually arrive as solutions. Find the **problem** underneath: the clash between what someone expected and what they found. Take the idea seriously: assume there is a real problem there, and dig until you have it.

1. **Look first.** Search, read files, check facts. Finding facts is your job; never ask the person for something you could look up.
2. **Name the clash** as a claim: what was expected, what happened instead, why it matters. Then list the three to five things the idea quietly assumes are true, fixed, or impossible.
3. **Ask, all at once.** Put the clash and the assumptions to the person as numbered questions, each with your recommended answer, worded so "yes" accepts it. Use the environment's question tool if it has one. Then stop and wait.
4. **Write it down.** Fill `## Problem` in `IDEA.md` in two or three plain sentences. Set `testable:` to whether any part of the idea makes a claim about what will actually happen in the world (`yes`, `partly`, or `no`).

Done when the person agrees, in their own words, that this is the problem they care about. A problem that just restates their idea isn't done.

**Reply:** the problem as written, any assumption that turned out wrong, and anything still open.
