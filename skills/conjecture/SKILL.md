---
name: conjecture
description: "Turn a problem into one bold idea that could be wrong, with its alternatives and what would show it wrong written first. Use when someone wants a real answer instead of options: 'what's your best guess', 'propose something', 'make the case'."
---

Call the Skill tool with "lexicon" if it isn't already loaded. Read `## Problem` in `IDEA.md`; the idea answers that problem and nothing wider.

A **conjecture** is one bold answer. Options and hedges are the absence of one.

1. **Find what's known.** Search and read: the usual answer, prior attempts, the evidence. This is the raw material, not the answer.
2. **Write the strongest alternative first,** the way its best supporter would. Usually it's the usual answer. Say where it falls short on this problem.
3. **Guess boldly, three times.** Three ideas that work in different ways, not three wordings of one. For each, run the **swap** on its parts. Drop any idea that a swap, a counterexample, or the problem itself knocks out. If more than one is left, keep the one that has stood up to the most, and move the others to `## Alternatives`.
4. **Say what would show it wrong** before anyone looks. For its testable parts, what you'd expect to see if it were false. For everything, a case it would get wrong and a way it could fail to solve the problem.
5. **Write it up** in `IDEA.md`: `## Idea`, `## Why it has to be this way` (each part, and what breaks if it's swapped), `## Alternatives`, `## What would show it wrong`. If one situation would tell the idea and the alternative apart, add it under `## Test` with both predictions. Set `status: criticised` once the first criticism lands; until then leave it `draft`.

Done when each part of the idea has survived a swap and at least one entry under "What would show it wrong" is something a person could actually go and check or construct.

**Reply:** the idea in a short paragraph, the alternative it beats and why, and what would show it wrong.
