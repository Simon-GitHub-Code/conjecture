A skill library can make AI help you find what's wrong with your idea, not just agree with it

The problem

More and more people explore ideas and learn by talking to an AI, and the AI's default is to agree and explain fluently. People expect the conversation to improve their idea; what they usually get back is a more confident version of the same idea. Coding skill libraries (pstack, Matt Pocock's skills) fixed the equivalent problem for code by leaning on checks that already existed, like tests and types. Idea work has no such check, so "be critical" is just a request the model forgets as the conversation grows.

The idea

A small set of skills can make criticism the normal outcome of exploring an idea with an AI, if it does four things. It steers the model with a few well-chosen words the model already understands (problem, conjecture, alternative, swap, refuter), rather than long instructions. It keeps the idea in one plain file the person can read. It runs a script that checks the file's shape: is there a problem, an alternative, something that would show it wrong, and is every criticism answered? It never asks whether the idea is true. And it turns that file into a page and a handoff, so other people and other AIs can find what the author can't. Everything the person reads is plain English.

Why it has to be this way

Swap any of these for something generic and the idea should break. If one swaps out with nothing else changing, that's a criticism.

Words the model already knowsan invented term has to be defined in every skill and drifts; "alternative" or "swap" pulls in the right behaviour in one word.
One plain filenotes in a chat can't be checked or handed on; a fixed shape can be.
A check on shape, never on truthan AI judging quality is the agreeable judge we're trying to get away from; a script checking shape is the one check that can't be talked round.
A criticism stays open until answeredwithout this, "I disagree" closes a criticism for free, and the idea survives without improving.
A fresh critic who hasn't seen the reasoninga critic who has heard the case for the idea tends to argue inside it.
A shareable pagethe criticism an idea needs most is the kind its author can't give.

Alternatives it has to beat

Custom instructions

tell the AI to be critical and play devil's advocate. Falls short because it fades as the conversation grows, keeps nothing between sessions, and nothing checks whether it happened.

Matt Pocock's grilling

a relentless interview that works through every decision. Gets the interview right (this plugin borrows it). Falls short because it has no standard for a good answer, so a bad idea can be grilled into a detailed bad idea.

A fully automated critique pipeline

the AI conjectures and criticises itself in rounds with no human in between. Falls short because the critic softens when nobody is watching, and an idea you didn't make the calls on isn't yours.

Veritula

a site where people post ideas and criticisms and the site shows which ideas have unanswered criticism. The strongest alternative, and the source of this plugin's open-criticism rule. Falls short for one person exploring an idea alone with an AI: it needs other people to show up.

What would show it wrong

Written before anyone went looking. Tick anything you know to be true, or a case you can construct.

The test

Setup

take five of the author's past idea conversations (ideas he still holds) and re-run each through /explore, alongside a plain chat with the instruction "be critical".

The idea predicts

at least two of the five change in a way that matters (an alternative is adopted, or a part of the idea is dropped), against none or one in the plain chat.

The alternative predicts

"be critical" produces as many real changes, so the skills add nothing.

Result: not run yet.

Criticism so far

A criticism stays open until the idea is revised to meet it, or someone answers it with a counter that nobody has answered in turn. The idea stands when nothing is open.

  1. C1The check becomes a ritualfixed by revising the idea

    The script checks shape, not substance, so the model will learn to fill the slots with plausible filler ("if users don't adopt it") and the check will pass empty records. Once the shape is the target, it stops measuring anything.

    Now rules out: an entry under "What would show it wrong" surviving the criticise step without someone actually trying to check or construct it; the critic's first job is to go and look for each one.

  2. C2Falsifiability only applies to scientific ideasfixed by revising the idea

    The first version demanded "falsifiers" for every idea, which is the popular misreading of Popper. Testability separates science from other knowledge; it doesn't separate good ideas from bad. Most ideas people bring (a business plan, a design, a moral question) can't be refuted by an observation, yet they can still be criticised.

    Now rules out: an untestable idea failing the check for having no observation that would refute it. The record says whether the idea is testable; for the parts that aren't, it is shown wrong by a counterexample, a contradiction, or failing to solve its own problem.

  3. C3"Withstood" was a free votefixed by revising the idea

    The person could mark any criticism "withstood" with no reason, and the idea moved on. That is a vote, and votes let ideas survive without improving (raised via Dennis Hackethal's work and Veritula).

    Now rules out: an idea being marked as standing while any criticism of it is unanswered. Rejecting a criticism now means writing a counter, which can itself be criticised.

  4. C4Too philosophical for most peoplefixed by revising the idea

    The library has to be useful to anyone. The early version had a 25-term glossary, strict prefixes and verdict types, and a record full of jargon. Most people will bounce off it.

    Now rules out: a person needing to learn any special vocabulary to read the record, the page, or the check's messages. The vocabulary is for steering the model and stays inside the skills.

  5. C5People don't want their ideas taken apartopen

    A tool whose normal result is "your idea has a problem" will be tried once and dropped. The records left behind will be the ideas that survived, so it will look like it works while quietly failing.

    1. C5.1You stay in chargeanswered

      Counter: It's opt-in, the person makes every call, and the criticism is answered in their own words. Being shown a flaw and choosing your own fix feels very different from being told you're wrong.

      1. C5.1.1That's a guess about feelingsstands

        Counter: C5.1 is an argument about how it will feel. Whether people keep using it is a question about behaviour, and only real use can answer it. Until the test has run, C5.1 doesn't settle anything.

Still unknown

  • Whether people come back to a tool that criticises their ideas (C5 stays open until the test runs).
  • Whether the steering words work as well on other models.
  • Whether the shape check catches empty rigour in practice, or only in principle.

Next questions

  • The person's answer is still the weak point: they can write a lazy counter. How do you make counters criticisable without taking the decision away from them?
  • Most people who explore ideas don't install plugins. Can the record travel into ordinary chat apps?
  • Can a record be posted to Veritula, so the people there criticise it?
